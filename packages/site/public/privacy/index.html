Privacy notice

What the Meltemi site, the sign-in service and Meltemi Diagrams keep about you, where it is kept, and how to have it removed.

Last updated: 24 October 2026

In short

  • This site sets no cookie, runs no analytics and has no forms.
  • The Meltemi IDE sends nothing to Meltemi and has no account.
  • Meltemi Diagrams works without an account. Then your work stays in your browser.
  • Signing in is optional, with GitHub or Google. Meltemi has no passwords.
  • You can see where you are signed in, and delete your account, at id.meltemi.dev/account.

Who runs this

Meltemi is run by its owner. The owner runs this site (meltemi.dev), the sign-in service (id.meltemi.dev) and the online service of Meltemi Diagrams (diagrams.meltemi.dev). The Meltemi IDE and the Meltemi Diagrams tools that you install run on your own computer.

Contact for any question about this notice, and for requests to see or to delete your data: Guillmar Ortiz, guillmar.ortiz@gmail.com

This site

This site is made of static files. It has no code of its own that could collect anything. It sets no cookie, runs no analytics and has no forms. Its pages load only their own stylesheet and images, and the policy the site sends to your browser forbids scripts and anything from other sites.

Some links lead to other sites, such as GitHub, which hosts the IDE's downloads. Their own notices apply there. The files of this site are served by Cloudflare; see Where the data is processed.

The Meltemi IDE

The Meltemi IDE runs on your computer. It has no account and sends no telemetry: every metric is computed locally and nothing is sent to Meltemi.

  • Its background service opens no network port. It listens only on a local endpoint that your user account alone can reach; reaching it from another machine is something you set up yourself, through an SSH tunnel.
  • It does not read, store or reuse the credentials of the coding agents you run from it. Each agent signs in by its own means, and the IDE only starts the agent's official program.
  • What it keeps stays on your computer: the logs of your sessions and of its service, in the data folder of your user account; its settings and permission rules, in the configuration folder; and the specs of each project, in a .meltemi folder inside that project. The data and configuration folders are %APPDATA%\meltemi\data and %APPDATA%\meltemi\config on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/meltemi (both) on macOS, and ~/.local/share/meltemi and ~/.config/meltemi on Linux.

The coding agents you run from it are their vendors' own programs. They run under their vendors' own terms and privacy notices, which this notice does not cover. More on the IDE page.

Signing in

Signing in is optional. It is needed only for the online features of Meltemi Diagrams. It goes through the identity service at id.meltemi.dev, and only with GitHub or Google. Meltemi has no passwords of its own.

When you choose a provider, the identity service reads your profile from it: your name, your picture, your email address and whether the provider says it is verified, your id at the provider, and your handle there if it has one (on GitHub, your username). It asks for read access only: the scopes read:user and user:email at GitHub, and openid, email and profile at Google.

The access token the provider gives is used once, to read that profile, and is then dropped. The identity service never keeps a provider token.

Your email address is also used to recognise you. A new sign-in joins an existing account only when the provider says the email is verified and an identity of that account had the same email verified within the last 90 days. Unverified emails never match. Otherwise you get a new account. You can also link and unlink providers yourself on the account page.

What the identity service holds

The identity service keeps four kinds of record, and nothing else:

Records of the identity service
Record What it holds
users A random user id, your name, the web address of your picture and when the account was created.
identities One for each provider you linked: the provider, your id there, your email address and whether the provider vouched for it, when it last did, your handle there and when you linked it.
sessions One for each place you are signed in: a session id, a coarse label such as “Edge on Windows”, when it started, when it was last seen, when it expires and whether it was ended.
events Deletion events waiting to be delivered to the products after an account is deleted: an event id, the product, a short message with your user id and the time, and the delivery attempts.

What the identity service never holds

  • A password: there are none.
  • A provider token: the one a provider gives is used once and dropped.
  • The raw identification your browser sends (the User-Agent): only the coarse label above is kept.
  • Analytics or telemetry of any kind.

It keeps no request log of its own with personal data, beyond the defaults of the platform it runs on. It does not store your picture, only its web address: your browser loads the picture from the provider's image host (GitHub or Google). A scheduled job removes sessions that ended more than seven days ago.

Cookies

This site sets no cookie. Signing in sets one cookie that stays and one that lasts only minutes.

meltemi_session

Set when you sign in. It lasts 30 days at most, or until you sign out. It is HttpOnly (scripts on a page cannot read it), Secure (sent over https only) and SameSite=Lax.

It is a domain cookie: your browser sends it to every address under meltemi.dev, which is how Meltemi Diagrams knows you are signed in. This site has no code and does not read it.

It holds a signed token: your user id, a session id, your name, the web address of your picture, your email address and whether it is verified, and when the token was issued and when it expires.

meltemi_oauth

Set for the few minutes a sign-in takes, and gone after ten minutes at most. It holds which provider you chose, where to send you back and random values that check the sign-in is yours. It is not used after you sign in.

These are the only two cookies that Meltemi's services set. Meltemi Diagrams also keeps data in your browser's own storage (localStorage and IndexedDB), which is not made of cookies: your work when you are not signed in, and a local copy of your online projects so that they open offline. Clearing your browser's site data removes it.

Your sessions and deleting your account

Open id.meltemi.dev/account while signed in. It shows the identities linked to your account (the provider, your handle or email there and when you linked it) and your sessions (where you are signed in, when each started, when it was last seen, and which one you are using now).

On that page you can link or unlink a provider, sign out of your other devices and delete your account. Signing out of a device ends its session at the identity service at once. Products check a session at most once an hour, so the change reaches them within an hour.

Deleting your account

You confirm by typing the word the page shows. Then the identity service:

  • deletes your user, your identities and your sessions in one step;
  • queues a deletion event for every product (today, Meltemi Diagrams);
  • clears your cookie.

Each product deletes the data it holds for you when it receives its event. The identity service sends the events at once and tries again for up to seven days if a product does not answer. The page that confirms the deletion says that the products delete their data within seven days.

The events stay in the identity service only while they are being delivered. An event holds a random event id, its type, your user id and the time, not your name or your email.

What Meltemi Diagrams holds

Without an account

The page works with no account, and your work stays in your browser. Share links made without an account carry the project in the part of the address after the # sign, which browsers do not send to servers. The page makes no request outside its own address. It asks the service once, at the start, whether you are signed in; with no session, the service keeps no account data about you.

Signed in

The service identifies you by your user id, the same one the identity service uses. It keeps:

  • your profile as the sign-in gave it: name, picture address, email address and whether it is verified, updated when it changes;
  • your projects: name, description, files (diagrams, other text files, images and fonts), a small preview image, and the state of the project's shared document;
  • the versions of each project: the ones you name, and one automatic version per day when something changed, with older automatic versions thinned out;
  • the sharing settings of each project: who can open it by its link, its members with their roles, and the invitations you made, each with the invited email address, the role, and when it was made;
  • your published catalogs, and the handle you chose for their addresses;
  • your API tokens, as a hash only, with the name you gave each one, when it was created and when it was last used (the token itself is shown once and not kept);
  • your usage: how many projects you have and how much storage their files take, to apply the limits;
  • the ids of the deletion events it has already handled.

The service can read your projects. They are not end-to-end encrypted: the service reads them to merge edits, to apply its limits and to show them to the people you choose.

Sharing and working together

New projects are private. The owner of a project chooses what its link allows: nothing (only the owner and the members), viewing, or editing. Anyone who holds the link of a project set to viewing or editing can open it, signed in or not; editing by link needs an account. The link is hard to guess, but it is not a password: whoever has it can pass it on.

Members of a project see each other's names and pictures. Only the owner sees the invitations and the email addresses on them. While several people have a project open, each sees the others' name, picture, open file and cursor. The service relays this between them and does not save it.

Published catalogs

Publishing is the project owner's choice. A published catalog is public: anyone can open it, without signing in, at an address made of your handle and the name you gave it. It is served from a domain separate from the app, and that domain sets no cookie. You can unpublish it at any time. If the owner of Meltemi takes a publication down, the service records which one, when and why.

Deleting

You can delete a project or a publication from the app at any time. Deleting your account deletes everything Meltemi Diagrams holds for you: when the deletion event arrives, the service removes your user record and API tokens, your projects with their versions, previews and shared documents, the memberships and invitations you created or that address your verified email, and your publications. Projects you own are deleted even if you shared them; if you are only a member of a project, you are removed from it and it stays.

The contents of stored files that nothing else uses are removed by a clean-up that runs weekly and touches only files older than seven days. They can therefore stay in storage for up to about two weeks after the rows that referred to them are gone.

Where the data is processed

This site, the identity service and the Meltemi Diagrams service are hosted on Cloudflare: the site as static files, the identity service on a Worker with a D1 database, and Meltemi Diagrams on Workers with D1 databases, R2 storage and Durable Objects. Cloudflare is the hosting provider, and the data described in this notice is stored and processed there.

When you sign in, GitHub or Google also process your sign-in on their side, under their own notices. The Meltemi IDE keeps everything on your computer.

Your choices and rights

You can ask for a copy of the data Meltemi holds about you, or ask for it to be deleted, by writing to the contact given under Who runs this.

You can also delete your account yourself at any time, as described above. You never need an account to use the IDE or the Meltemi Diagrams web app.
